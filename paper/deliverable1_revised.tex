\begin{abstract}
\noindent Independent reinforcement-learning market makers settle at spreads above the competitive level without communicating. A wide spread is either \emph{rent} that an entrant can capture or the correct price of \emph{toxic} order flow, and the two look identical on the tape. We study the entrant's problem on Hyperliquid, an on-chain order book where every print carries both counterparties' wallet addresses. This document revises our October~6 proposal in light of three days of simulation and the first Hyperliquid data. Three things changed. (1)~The exploitability hypothesis ``more collusive $\Rightarrow$ more exploitable'' holds only in a trivial form; the fraction of rent a profit-maximising entrant destroys is flat at $\approx 0.7$ across populations, so RQ2 is restated as a measurement. (2)~The mechanism by which wallet identity pays is not wallet persistence (same-wallet repeats are $3$--$4\times$ chance but only $0.02$--$0.05$ in level); it is that informed flow clusters in time and a toxic wallet's print reveals the regime \emph{before the price does}. The simulator now has a latent toxicity regime and a lag between a print and its mark, and with them identity's value reappears where the data say it should. (3)~The quoted spread on BTC, ETH and SOL is pinned at one tick essentially all the time, so on those books rent cannot live in the spread; it can only live in which fills a maker gets. A fill at the touch pays that one tick and loses $0.3$--$0.6$ bps to the mid within ten seconds on every coin; the taker's cost is in depth---one order in ten walks the book and pays twenty ticks, and on the deep books those are the informed ones. We lay out the revised model, the results that forced the revisions, the calibration numbers, and the plan to a December preprint.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and central claim}
Market makers (MMs) post bids and asks, earn the spread from impatient traders, and lose to \emph{informed} traders whose orders predict the next price move. Increasingly they are learning algorithms. Three facts make this a trading-firm problem rather than only a regulator's. (i)~Independent learning MMs drift to spreads above the competitive level without communicating \cite{contxiong,cfl}. (ii)~A spread above competitive is rent an entrant can capture by undercutting, but the same wide spread is also the correct response to toxic flow: identical observable, opposite action. (iii)~Optiver reports that current LLM traders name adverse selection yet trade at negative expected value and fail to model how others react \cite{optiver}. The skill a desk wants---read the tape, decide whether competitors are soft or the flow is poisonous, and quote accordingly without becoming the exploited party---has no quantitative benchmark.

\paragraph{Central claim, revised.} Learning market makers earn supra-competitive rents that survive entry: a simple entrant that reads markouts off the public tape takes most of the rent and keeps it when the incumbents are allowed to re-learn, because the cartel re-forms around the entrant rather than collapsing. The entrant's problem is therefore not whether rent exists but \emph{which flow to take}, and that is a regime-inference problem. On a venue with public wallet identity, the identity of the last taker reveals the toxicity regime before the price move that would reveal it anonymously; the lag between a print and its mark is where the value of identity lives. On tick-constrained books, where the spread cannot widen, the same fill-selection mechanism is the only place rent can live at all.

The pieces exist separately: learned rents \cite{cfl,contxiong}; strategic exploitation of learners in abstract games \cite{dss,brownmackay}; on Hyperliquid, persistent wallet histories carry adverse-selection information \cite{zhai} and disclosed flow changes impact \cite{barone}. What is new here is the chain: learned rent $\to$ entrant best response $\to$ value of public identity, measured in the same units in the simulator and on the venue.

\section{Model}\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Market}
One contract, discrete time $t=1,\dots,T$ (one block per period, $T=100$ per episode), $N$ quoting MMs on a tick grid of size $\theta$. Each MM $i$ posts symmetric quotes at half-spread $h^i_t\in\{1,\dots,K\}$ ticks around the mid $m_t$. One taker arrives per period: informed with probability $\alpha$, else a liquidity trader with private valuation $m_t+L$, $L\sim N(0,\sigma_L^2)$, who buys if the best ask is below her valuation and sells if the best bid is above it. The fundamental is $V_t = m_t \pm d$, $d\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\mu)$, redrawn each period; an informed taker trades only when $d$ exceeds the best half-spread, so no quote is immune and, by memorylessness, an informed fill costs the maker exactly $\mu$ in expectation at every spread. (Deliverable~1's binary-$V$ specification had an adverse-selection cliff---any half-spread beyond the edge was immune---which tabular learners find and sit on; the exponential edge removes it while keeping closed forms.) The taker hits the best quote; ties split uniformly; the maker's profit per fill is $(p-V_t)$ on a sale, $(V_t-p)$ on a purchase.

\paragraph{Benchmarks (closed form).} With $x=h\theta$, the market-wide expected one-period profit of quoting $h$ is
\[
\Pi(h;\alpha)=(1-\alpha)\,2x\,\bar\Phi(x/\sigma_L)\;-\;\alpha\,\mu\,e^{-x/\mu},
\]
linear in $\alpha$. The competitive half-spread $h^C$ is the first grid point with $\Pi\ge0$ (Bertrand undercutting stops there), the joint-monopoly $h^M$ maximises $\Pi$, and $\Pi^C,\Pi^M$ are the profits at those points. The collusion index of a population is $\Delta=(\bar\Pi-\Pi^C)/(\Pi^M-\Pi^C)$ \cite{calvano}; we write $C(\pi)$ for the same quantity when the population is frozen and evaluated. With $\theta=0.5$, $K=24$, $\sigma_L=8$, $\mu=5$: $(h^C,h^M)=(2,13)$ at $\alpha=0.1$, $(4,14)$ at $0.3$, $(8,17)$ at $0.5$; $\Pi^M-\Pi^C$ falls from $1.90$ to $0.66$, so less rent is available where flow is more toxic.

\subsection{Takers with identity: types, regime, and the mark lag}\label{sec:takers}
This subsection replaces the ``persistent wallet id'' sentence of Deliverable~1 and is the main modelling change.

\paragraph{Types.} A population of $M$ wallets ($M=50$) with persistent types $\alpha_j$, taken as stratified quantiles of $\mathrm{Beta}(\bar\alpha\kappa_\alpha,(1-\bar\alpha)\kappa_\alpha)$ so that the mean is pinned at $\bar\alpha$ and the concentration $\kappa_\alpha$ sets only the dispersion ($\kappa_\alpha\to\infty$ recovers the homogeneous model; the subscript keeps it apart from the fraction of rent destroyed $\kappa_A$). Wallet $j$'s order is informed with probability $\alpha_j$.

\paragraph{Regime.} A latent state $z_t\in\{\text{calm},\text{toxic}\}$, kept with probability $\rho_z$ and otherwise redrawn from a stationary law chosen so the stationary mean toxicity equals $\bar\alpha$. Wallet activity depends on the state: in the toxic state arrival weights are $\propto w_j\alpha_j$, in the calm state $\propto w_j(1-\alpha_j)$. With $\kappa_\alpha=1$ the two states have toxicity $0.15$ and $0.65$ around $\bar\alpha=0.3$. The identity of the last print is therefore evidence about the regime the \emph{next} arrival shares, whoever it is. (A separate same-wallet persistence parameter $\rho$ remains in the code; the data put it near zero and it is off in all results below.)

\paragraph{Public information and the mark lag.} After commitment, a print is public: event, price, and wallet id. Its \emph{mark}---the ex-post value $V_t$ against which the fill's profit is measured---becomes public $L$ periods later (\texttt{mark\_lag}). Deliverable~1 implicitly had $L=1$, which gives an anonymous observer oracle-quality markouts before the next arrival. On Hyperliquid the next order arrives in $0.4$--$1.8$\,s and markouts realise over seconds to minutes; $L$ is the number of arrivals that elapse before the tape reveals what identity revealed at the print. Information sets are nested:
\[
\Fanon_t \subset \Fid_t \subset \For_t ,
\]
no ids / committed wallet ids / the true types. Nobody ever sees the identity of the order about to hit them.

\subsection{Agents}
\paragraph{Incumbents.} Tabular Q-learning \cite{calvano,cfl}: state $=$ (own last half-spread, min rival last half-spread), action $=$ half-spread, $\varepsilon$-greedy with $\varepsilon_t=e^{-\beta t}$, learning rate $\eta$, discount $0.95$, $2\times10^4$ episodes. Incumbents are identity-blind by construction, so the identity experiments measure what identity is worth to a newcomer against an identity-blind cartel. ``Re-learning'' incumbents resume Q-learning after entry with a fresh exploration schedule.

\paragraph{Entrants.} All see only public information.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Competitive-GM}: quotes $h^C(\bar\alpha)$, ignores everyone. The textbook benchmark.
\item \emph{One-tick undercut}: one tick inside the best incumbent quote. The myopic upper bound if nobody punishes.
\item \emph{Own-fill markout} (Deliverable~1's ``markout rule''): estimates toxicity from the sign of its own fills' profits and undercuts when the implied rent is positive.
\item \emph{Wallet entrant} under $\Fanon$, $\Fid$ or $\For$: exact grid-Bayes estimation of $\bar\alpha$ from every period (print or not, since the best quote is public) and of each wallet's $\alpha_j$ from its prints with a print-conditional likelihood (no fill-selection bias; after 300 episodes the population estimate is within $0.005$ of truth and per-wallet error $0.03$). A two-state filter tracks $b_t=P(z_t=\text{toxic})$: with ids the evidence at a print is \emph{who} printed (likelihood ratio $\alpha_j(1-\bar\alpha)/((1-\alpha_j)\bar\alpha)$); without ids the evidence at the print is only that a print occurred, and the sign of its profit arrives $L$ periods later and is carried to the present through $\rho_z^{L}$ (the keep-or-redraw chain is reversible, so this is exact). Forecast $\alpha_{\mathrm{next}}=b\,\alpha(\text{toxic})+(1-b)\,\alpha(\text{calm})$. Quote rule: with best incumbent quote $b^*$, undercut to $\min(b^*-1,\,h^M(\alpha_{\mathrm{next}}))$ when $b^*-1\ge h^C(\alpha_{\mathrm{next}})$, else quote $h^C(\alpha_{\mathrm{next}})$, which for a toxic forecast is wider than the incumbents and amounts to withdrawing.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Statistics}
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Delta$ / $C(\pi)$ as above; a \emph{mechanism test}: in states where a rival quotes at or inside the monopoly point, is the learner's greedy action its myopic best response (undercut) or restraint? Restraint share, forgone one-period reward, and Q-gap are reported.
\item Exploitability: $\Pi_{\mathrm{nominal}}$ (frozen incumbents alone), $J(\pi;A)$ (incumbents' profit with attacker $A$), $L^A_{\mathrm{BR}}=\Pi_{\mathrm{nominal}}-J$, $L_{\mathrm{BR}}=\max_A L^A_{\mathrm{BR}}$, and $\kappa_A=L^A_{\mathrm{BR}}/(\Pi_{\mathrm{nominal}}-\Pi^C)$, the fraction of rent destroyed. Entrant profit is reported in rent units $(\Pi^M-\Pi^C)$ so $N$ and $\alpha$ are comparable.
\item Value of information: $\pi^{\mathrm{id}}-\pi^{\mathrm{anon}}$ and $\pi^{\mathrm{oracle}}-\pi^{\mathrm{id}}$, paired by seed and market realisation (common random numbers).
\item Adverse-selection shift: informed share of the entrant's fills vs the incumbents' fills vs the market without the entrant.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What is deliberately simple, and what the data say about it}
Unit size, symmetric quotes, risk-neutral makers, exogenous taker mix, no inventory in the state (inventory buckets are implemented and off), no fees or rebates, no request budgets, no liquidation flow. Two of these are now known to matter on the venue. The spread on the major books is one tick (\S\ref{sec:l2}), so the symmetric-half-spread action does not describe BTC/ETH/SOL under normal conditions; it describes thin books (HYPE opens to two ticks) and stress. And the archive records each counterparty's pre-trade position on every print, so inventory paths of real MM wallets are observable and the inventory-skew benchmark can be calibrated rather than assumed.

\section{Hyperliquid: mechanics and data}\label{sec:hl}
\paragraph{Mechanics as treatment arms} (unchanged from Deliverable~1 except where noted). Persistent pseudonymous wallet addresses on every committed order and both sides of every print. Per-user request budgets earned by filling, so the only ``drain'' attack is \emph{induced self-exhaustion} of a reactive rival. A priority fee that buys $\approx45$\,ms/bp for aggressive orders and queue position for resting ones \cite{hldocs}. A price grid of at most five significant figures: at current prices one tick is \$1 on BTC, \$0.10 on ETH, \$0.01 on SOL, \$0.001 on HYPE, i.e.\ $0.12$, $0.39$, $0.87$ and $0.11$\,bps respectively---and \S\ref{sec:l2} shows the spread sits on it. A protocol vault (HLP) with undisclosed strategies, which we treat as a calibration target for a fixed-policy benchmark, not a known policy. Liquidation flow routed to the book, with the October~10, 2025 cascade as the stress case.

\paragraph{Archive.} Albers et al.\ \cite{albers}, Zenodo record 18184441: trades for all perpetuals October 2025--January 2026 (JSON lines, both counterparties with pre-trade position, order ids, TWAP flags); order lifecycles and book diffs for BTC/ETH/SOL in December 2025. Two facts not in the schema document that matter for use: \texttt{side\_info} is $[\text{buyer},\text{seller}]$ (verified on 3.4M trades by the order-id rule: the aggressor's order is the newer one in 97.1\% of trades; the other 2.9\% are aggressors \emph{older} than the resting order, which is what triggered stop and liquidation orders look like, and are flagged); and the L1 hash is all zeros for orders filled in their submission block, so it cannot be an order key---fills of one sweep are collapsed on (block time, taker, side). January coverage is Jan 1--3 and Jan 19--31; we used Jan 26--28 (72\,h, 3.42M prints on BTC/ETH/SOL/HYPE, 1.44M taker orders).

\paragraph{Live feed.} A recorder on the public WebSocket (\texttt{data/record.py}) writes trades with both wallets, L2 snapshots, and the top of book on every change. 24\,h of trades (Oct 6--7, 871k prints) and 6\,h with the book (Oct 8). The feed and the archive agree on every structural statistic (\S\ref{sec:data}).

\paragraph{Pipeline.} \texttt{analysis/calibrate.py} turns either source into one row per coin: activity, markouts (proxy and true-mid), same-taker repeat rate at the order level against its random-matching baseline, split-half rank correlation of per-taker markouts, the conditional markout after a toxic vs a benign wallet's order, the same split conditioned on the anonymous ``price already moved against the last maker'' signal, MM wallet classification with a shuffled-label null for turn-taking, and the book statistics. Classification always uses the first half of a sample and measurement the second.

\section{Results to date}\label{sec:results}
All simulator results: $\theta=0.5$, $K=24$, $\sigma_L=8$, $\mu=5$, common random numbers across policies within a cell, means over seeds with seed-level standard deviations where quoted.

\subsection{Replication of learned supra-competitive spreads}
Two or three Q-learners ($\eta=0.15$, $\beta=4\times10^{-6}$, 10 seeds) settle at half-spreads of 10.6, 14.4 and 17.4 ticks for $\alpha=0.1,0.3,0.5$ against competitive 2, 4, 8 and monopoly 13, 14, 17: $\Delta=0.83$, $0.86$, $0.77$ (seed sd $0.01$--$0.02$). A robustness grid at $\alpha=0.3$ over $N\in\{2,3,5\}$, $\eta\in\{0.05,0.15\}$ and optimistic/pessimistic initialisation gives $\Delta=0.71$--$0.87$ in every cell (Table~\ref{tab:robust}); the slow learners ($\eta=0.05$) quote tighter. The mechanism test says this is restraint, not under-exploration: in 55--83\% of visited states a rival sits at or inside the monopoly quote, and in 96--100\% of those the learner's greedy action forgoes the myopic undercut. The best-quoter identity switches between episodes 45--70\% of the time: a turn-taking convention, not a fixed leader.

\begin{itemize}
\item The literal hypothesis holds, by construction. The attacker that minimises incumbent profit is the competitive-GM quoter in 179 of 180 populations, and it pushes the incumbents \emph{below} $\Pi^C$ (frozen incumbents sometimes respond to a quote at $h^C$ by undercutting it and lose), so $L_{\mathrm{BR}}\approx\Pi_{\mathrm{nominal}}$ and the slope is one ($\kappa_A=1.14$ on average, and slightly \emph{decreasing} in $C$: $-0.10\ [-0.14,-0.06]$ at $\alpha=0.3$).
\item The substantive version is not supported. $\kappa_A$, the fraction of rent a profit-maximising attacker destroys, is $0.71$--$0.73$ for undercut, markout and oracle attackers across the whole range of $C$. Pooled and within-cell slopes include zero; at $\alpha=0.3$ the OLS slope is $+0.23$ to $+0.25$ with intervals that just exclude zero while the rank correlation does not ($\rho=0.08$--$0.11$, $p>0.17$), which over the observed range of $C$ is at most $0.13$ in $\kappa_A$. Higher-rent populations are exploitable in proportion to their rent, with at most a weak excess.
\item Damage and profit are different objectives. The attacker that hurts the incumbents most earns least ($0.10$ rent units); the ones that earn most (markout $0.51$, oracle $0.49$, undercut $0.48$) leave the incumbents 27--30\% of their rent. The markout rule is the attacker's own best response in 89 of 180 populations, the undercutter in 58, the oracle in 33.
\item \emph{Train at $\bar\alpha$, test at $\bar\alpha'$.} Evaluating every cartel on markets of toxicity $0.1$, $0.3$ and $0.5$ (60 cartels, 500 episodes): a cartel trained on more toxic flow keeps $C=0.80$--$0.86$ and $\kappa_A=0.65$--$0.75$ on any calmer market, because a wide quote is still a collusive quote there; as toxicity rises past the training level the fixed quote approaches the new break-even ($C=0.69$ for the $0.3$-cartel on the $0.5$ market, $0.23$ for the $0.1$-cartel; never below $\Pi^C$ on its own) and the entrant's undercut then pushes the thin-rent cartel below the competitive benchmark ($\kappa_A=2.0$--$2.5$ for the $0.1$-cartel on the $0.5$ market). $\kappa_A\approx0.7$ is a property of cartels whose rent is intact, not of the training toxicity; the vulnerable case is a cartel caught by a regime it was not trained for.
\item Knowing the true $\alpha$ adds nothing over estimating it from own fills ($0.49$ vs $0.51$). In a stationary market the public tape carries the full value of the toxicity \emph{level}. This is the observation that sent us to heterogeneous takers.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Entry: frozen and re-learning incumbents (RQ1, first form)}
Against frozen two-maker cartels, the own-fill markout entrant captures 79\%, 96\% and 93\% of the monopoly rent at $\alpha=0.1,0.3,0.5$ (entrant profit $0.95$, $0.72$, $0.36$ per period); the one-tick undercutter 84\%, 89\%, 75\%; the competitive quoter 18\%, 8\%, 14\%. The entrant wins 54--62\% of fills, not all of them: it wins the fills at profitable prices.

With incumbents that resume learning ($\varepsilon_0=0.3$ decaying to $0$ over 8000 episodes, Figure~\ref{fig:relearn}), the three policies produce three outcomes. The competitive entrant collapses the cartel: market $\Delta\to0$, the best quote sits at $h^C$, the entrant wins every fill and earns exactly $\Pi(h^C)$, and the re-learning incumbents converge to zero profit. The undercut and markout entrants let the cartel \emph{re-form around them}: $\Delta$ rises as exploration decays ($0.28\to0.62$ at $\alpha=0.5$ for markout), the best quote drifts wider, and the entrant's final profit equals its frozen-incumbent profit ($0.95/0.67/0.39$ vs $0.95/0.72/0.36$). Collapsing the cartel is the worst thing an entrant can do to itself: quoting at $h^C$ leaves 59\%, 82\% and 72\% of the markout entrant's profit on the table. Inference beats blind undercutting at every $\alpha$ and every stage, most during exploration ($\alpha=0.5$, first window: $0.25$ vs $0.03$), because the undercutter follows exploring incumbents into loss-making quotes.

\subsection{The value of identity in the simulator (RQ1, revised form)}\label{sec:identity}
Three grids against frozen two-maker cartels, ten seeds (one trained cartel per seed), entrant profit in rent units, break-even quote rule. Differences between information sets are paired within a seed (same cartel, common random numbers) and reported with 95\% intervals over seeds; the level of entrant profit varies by $\pm0.05$--$0.1$ across seeds with the cartel the seed trained, so unpaired comparisons would hide everything below.

\paragraph{(a) Wallet persistence (the Deliverable-1 mechanism).} With same-wallet persistence $\rho$ and type concentration $\kappa_\alpha$, identity is worth exactly nothing at $\rho=0$ or $\kappa_\alpha=\infty$, and $+0.15$ to $+0.30$ rent units at $\rho=0.9$--$0.99$, $\kappa_\alpha=1$, $\bar\alpha\in\{0.3,0.5\}$ (intervals $\pm0.05$ to $\pm0.14$; ten of ten seeds positive at $\rho=0.9$); at $\bar\alpha=0.1$ it is $+0.03$ to $+0.07$ and not distinguishable from zero. The identity entrant attains the oracle (oracle $-$ id within $\pm0.04$). The mechanism is fill selection: at $\bar\alpha=0.5$, $\rho=0.99$ it withdraws in 31\% of periods, its fills are 18\% informed against the incumbents' 53\%, and the identity-blind cartel's residual book goes below $\Pi^C$ while market $\Delta$ stays at $0.9$---the rent moved to the entrant (Figure~\ref{fig:as}). The data put $\rho$ near $0.03$, so this channel is the theory check, not the mechanism.

\paragraph{(c) Regime with the mark lag.} Table~\ref{tab:lag} and Figure~\ref{fig:lag}. At $\bar\alpha=0.5$ the anonymous entrant's profit falls as the mark arrives later ($0.95\to0.84$ at $\rho_z=0.99$) while the identity entrant's stays at $1.00$--$1.01$, so identity's increment grows from $+0.06$ to $+0.16$ ($\pm0.07$) at $\rho_z=0.99$ and from $+0.06$ to $+0.11$ ($\pm0.05$, peak at $L=20$) at $\rho_z=0.9$; nine or ten of ten seeds positive in every cell. The peak is informative: at $\rho_z=0.9$ a regime lives about ten arrivals, and a mark that is sixty arrivals late is no worse than one twenty late because the tape was already useless at twenty. Identity's value grows with the mark lag as long as the lag is shorter than the regime's lifetime. At $\bar\alpha=0.3$ the increment is $+0.02$ to $+0.04$, distinguishable from zero only at $\rho_z=0.99$ and $L\ge20$ (ten of ten at $L=60$): against $\sim11$-tick incumbents even the toxic state usually leaves an undercut profitable, so the regime is rarely decision-relevant. At $\bar\alpha=0.1$ it is zero or slightly negative. The value of the wallet tag to an inside quoter is largest where the resting spread is close to the toxic-regime break-even---a prediction about \emph{which} books reward identity-aware quoting, and the reason the one-tick books of \S\ref{sec:l2}, where the spread cannot move at all, are where identity should matter most (\S\ref{sec:queue}).

\paragraph{(d) Where information hurts.} At $\kappa_\alpha=5$ the types lie within $0.2$ of each other, a wallet's identity carries little regime information, and the mean increment is zero---but the seed distribution has a tail: in three of ten cartels at $\bar\alpha=0.3$, $\rho_z=0.9$, $L=20$ the anonymous entrant out-earns both the identity and the oracle entrant by $0.2$--$0.5$ rent units. In those cartels the anonymous entrant's steady undercut takes 86--99\% of fills and the incumbents earn nothing; the better-informed entrants' quotes move with their regime belief, and the incumbents' learned reaction takes back 26--33\% of the fills. An entrant that uses information naively can provoke the defenders. The reaction function is part of the game---the steering channel of RQ3, met here by accident---and the reason every identity number in this document is reported with its minimum over seeds.

\subsubsection{The book}\label{sec:l2}
Two book windows: six hours on October~8 from the throttled \texttt{l2Book} feed (one snapshot per $5.4$\,s) and six hours on October~11--12 from the \texttt{bbo} feed (every top-of-book change; median gap $0.13$\,s). Two alignment rules were needed, both learned from the second window and both now in \texttt{analysis/l2.py}: a trade and the top-of-book update it causes carry the same block timestamp, so a fill's reference mid is the last snapshot \emph{strictly before} the trade (at-or-before returns the post-trade book and gave a negative effective spread on BTC); and marks are computed only inside the recorder's coverage window. Table~\ref{tab:l2} is the second window; the first window's ten-second markouts are kept as a row for comparison.

\paragraph{The quote is one tick; the cost is in depth.} The quoted spread is one tick on BTC, ETH and SOL in both windows (p90 $=$ median). A fill at the pre-trade touch pays exactly the quoted spread---effective $0.12$ / $0.42$ / $0.17$ / $0.92$ bps against quoted $0.12$ / $0.39$ / $0.12$ / $0.90$---which is also the check that the alignment is right. But 20--46\% of prints are not at the touch: one order in ten (one in five on HYPE) walks past it, and those prints pay $2.4$--$5.2$ bps, twenty ticks on BTC. Print-weighted, the effective spread is $0.73$ / $1.01$ / $1.73$ / $1.76$ bps, six times the quoted spread on BTC and fifteen on HYPE; equal-weighted across orders (the order's volume-weighted price against the pre-trade mid) it is $0.27$ / $0.60$ / $0.40$ / $1.09$, $1.2$--$3.4\times$ quoted. On a one-tick book the maker's price decision is not the inside quote but how much to show at the inside and behind it.

\paragraph{The ordinary fill loses, and it is not a sweep artefact.} Against the fresh mid the maker is under water at one second on BTC, ETH and HYPE ($-0.26$ / $-0.30$ / $-0.19$ bps; SOL $+0.15$) and at ten seconds on all four ($-0.52$ / $-0.92$ / $-0.25$ / $-0.02$). The October~8 level---$-0.30$ to $-0.36$ on every coin---did not reproduce; the sign did, on every coin in both windows, and in both windows the adverse move exceeds the half-spread earned ($0.06$ / $0.20$ / $0.06$ / $0.45$). Splitting by where the print landed settles whether large sweeps drive this: fills \emph{at the touch}, the fill a quiet maker gets, lose $-0.41$ / $-0.56$ / $-0.45$ / $-0.27$ bps at ten seconds on all four coins. Walked fills split by book. On the deep BTC and ETH books the makers an order walks to lose more than the touch ($-0.80$, $-2.26$): an order that pays twenty ticks to walk BTC is informed and its impact is permanent. On HYPE and SOL walked fills break even or earn ($-0.01$, $+0.98$): walking a \$6k-deep or $0.9$-bps-wide book is cheap and uninformative, the price reverts, and the maker resting deep keeps the walk. This is the regime contrast of \S\ref{sec:takers} measured by depth instead of by wallet. The identity split against the true mid holds on BTC, HYPE and SOL (after toxic minus after benign $-0.70$ / $-0.48$ / $-0.47$ bps) and is $+0.06$ on ETH at six hours of marked fills; the 125.8-hour trades-only split ($-0.66$ / $-1.02$ / $-0.52$ / $-0.63$ given the anonymous signal) is the one to quote for identity.

\paragraph{Who earns the spread.} Every marked fill carries the maker's wallet, so the RQ3 anchor can be read directly (Figure~\ref{fig:makers}; two recording sessions eight days apart, 15 hours, 112k / 55k / 106k / 29k marked fills; the top-10 MM wallets are chosen by volume over the full 327-hour trade window, not on the hours being scored; SEs cluster by five-minute block). The hierarchy the simulator predicts is in the data: makers the classifier does not call MMs lose $-0.41$ to $-1.25$ bps per fill at ten seconds, the other MMs $-0.12$ to $-0.53$, and the top-10 MMs break even or earn ($-0.10$ / $+0.19$ / $+0.11$ / $+0.52$ on BTC/ETH/HYPE/SOL, holding 9--24\% of fills). Restricting to fills \emph{at the touch}---the same price, the same tick---the top-10 earn $0.4$--$1.1$ bps per fill more than everyone else ($-0.02$ / $+0.24$ / $+0.09$ / $+0.52$ against $-0.41$ / $-0.70$ / $-0.56$ / $-0.56$): makers resting at the same price get different fills, and the difference is \emph{when} they are at the front. Part of the edge is not being swept (top-10 touch share $0.70$--$0.94$ against $0.43$--$0.79$). The skill is persistent across days, not a calm spell: makers with at least fifty marked fills on each of the two nights keep their ranking (Spearman $0.75$ / $0.50$ / $0.43$ / $0.56$, $n=108$ / $65$ / $68$ / $45$, $p<10^{-3}$). Eight to fifteen makers per coin have a significantly positive realized spread and they take 8--30\% of all fills; the rest of the book loses to the mid within ten seconds. Makers who select earn the spread; identity-blind resting liquidity is the counterparty of that rent---the selection cartel of \S\ref{sec:queue}, observed.

\paragraph{They do not withdraw; they are absent from the wallet.} \emph{How} the top makers select can be read from the same fills. The venue lever of \S\ref{sec:anonymity} predicts that incumbents who read the regime step out of the inside after toxic flow. They do not. The top-10 share of touch fills in a block against the mid's absolute 10-s move per fill in the \emph{previous} block is flat on ETH, SOL and HYPE at 30-s, 60-s and 5-minute blocks (Spearman $-0.07$ to $+0.03$) and \emph{positive} on BTC ($+0.13$ at every block length, $p<10^{-3}$); at fill level, against the mid's move over the ten seconds before the order arrived, the terciles are $0.097/0.095/0.123$ (BTC), $0.248/0.272/0.247$ (ETH), $0.282/0.290/0.275$ (SOL), $0.212/0.246/0.240$ (HYPE). Yet their selection is real and contemporaneous: within the same 30-s block their share falls as that block's realized toxicity (the adverse selection of everyone else's fills, excluding their own) rises (ETH $-0.09$, SOL $-0.12$, $p<10^{-3}$), and their markout edge over the rest holds or widens when the tape is stressed (ETH $0.94\to0.99$, SOL $0.95\to1.31$, HYPE $0.55\to0.73$ bps; BTC narrows, $0.56\to0.26$). They are absent from the fills that turn out toxic without having left the touch, and nothing in the public tape's recent move predicts when.

What predicts it is the wallet (Figure~\ref{fig:idsel}). Typing every taker with at least thirty touch fills on the \emph{first} night by the markout it inflicted (rank terciles), and scoring the \emph{second} night: the typing holds (last night's toxic takers cost the maker $1.0$ / $1.7$ / $2.3$ / $1.2$ bps more than the benign ones on BTC/ETH/SOL/HYPE, and they are 20--27\% of all touch fills), and the top-10 makers' share of their fills is $0.027$ / $0.059$ / $0.121$ / $0.118$ against $0.121$ / $0.383$ / $0.422$ / $0.301$ of the benign takers' and $0.133$ / $0.308$ / $0.388$ / $0.253$ of unseen takers'---a factor $2.6$--$6.5$, Spearman of ``filled by a top maker'' with the taker's past toxicity $-0.17$ to $-0.33$, $p<10^{-3}$ on every coin. The gap is the same inside calm tape ($0.158/0.027$, $0.350/0.049$, $0.471/0.116$, $0.263/0.115$) and inside stressed tape ($0.115/0.036$, $0.400/0.072$, $0.387/0.132$, $0.302/0.112$): it is the wallet, not the moment. Speed explains part of it but not all: among fills after which the mid did not move within one second---not pick-offs, 37--61\% of fills---the top makers still take $0.087$ / $0.151$ / $0.180$ / $0.238$ of the toxic takers' fills against $0.141$ / $0.424$ / $0.478$ / $0.300$ of the benign takers', and those quiet toxic fills still lose $0.3$--$0.8$ bps at ten seconds, so the wallet's information plays out over seconds and the tag is worth having before the tape shows it (the mark-lag mechanism of \S\ref{sec:identity}). Never being stale is most of the avoidance on pick-offs ($0.006$ vs $0.067$ on BTC, $0.038$ vs $0.259$ on ETH); avoiding the wallet is the rest. Identity-conditioned fill selection is operating on the real book, and it is RQ1's empirical anchor: the value of the wallet tag is realized by the makers who earn the spread, through which fills they are not in.

\subsection{The one-tick queue game (RQ3, first result)}\label{sec:queue}
Section~\ref{sec:l2} says the spread on the major books cannot move. We therefore built the game in which it does not: the spread is fixed at $h$ ticks; each period every maker chooses to \emph{rest} at the inside or \emph{step out}; resting makers form a time-priority queue; the taker hits the front for one unit; the filled maker's order is consumed and it rejoins at the back if it keeps resting, so fills rotate among those who stay; a maker that steps out and returns joins at the back. Takers, wallet types, regime and mark lag are those of \S\ref{sec:takers}. The expected value of a fill at $h$ against a taker of type $\alpha$ is $(1-q)x-q\mu$ with $q$ the fill-conditional informed probability; at the population break-even $h=h^C$ it is $+0.15$ on average, $+1.10$ in the calm state and $-2.26$ in the toxic one ($\kappa_\alpha=1$): all the value is in selection. One tick above break-even ($+0.58$ average) a coarse tick leaves the inside slightly supra-competitive and the queue shares that rent.

The incumbents are two always-resting makers---the tick-constrained competitive book---and the entrant is the wallet entrant with the decision ``rest iff the fill value against the regime forecast is positive''. Figure~\ref{fig:queue} and Table~\ref{tab:queue}.

\begin{itemize}
\item At break-even the always-resting maker earns $\approx0$, as the BTC book data say the average resting fill does, and the identity entrant earns $0.14$--$0.16$ per period by resting only when the regime is calm. Its fills are 16\% informed against the incumbents' 29--31\%, and the incumbents' profit goes to zero or below: the toxic flow is left to those who stay.
\item The anonymous selector's edge collapses with the mark lag ($+0.06$ over always-resting at $L=1$, $+0.01$ at $L=20$, $\rho_z=0.9$) while the identity entrant's does not ($+0.10$). A tape that is twenty arrivals stale cannot time the regime; identity can. The identity entrant attains 90--100\% of the oracle.
\item One tick above break-even the rotation shares the tick rent ($0.15$ each) and identity adds about 45\% to it ($0.21$), taken from the incumbents ($0.14\to0.11$).
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Learned incumbents.} Two things had to change for incumbents that learn. First, a \emph{fringe}: two always-resting makers in the population, the retail limit orders and the vault that never step out. Without them two learners that both step out in a toxic spell leave an empty book, no prints, a frozen tape signal, and a self-fulfilling lock in which nobody ever rests again. Second, the learner. Tabular Q-learning cannot learn to queue: with time priority the reward of resting arrives only after several consecutive periods in line, an $\epsilon$-greedy learner breaks the chain, never reaches the front, sees zero whether it stays or leaves, and flickers (at 12{,}000 episodes it rests 40--60\% of periods and holds 0--4\% of fills; rank in the state and optimistic initialisation do not help). The proposal's other incumbent class does: a no-regret learner (EXP3) over complete queue policies $\{$always rest, never rest, anonymous selector$\}$ converges in a few hundred episodes. \begin{itemize}
\item \emph{What the incumbents learn.} When the tape is fresh ($L\le5$, or $\rho_z=0.99$ at any lag) both incumbents become anonymous selectors (expert weight $0.9$--$1.0$); when it is stale ($L\ge20$ at $\rho_z=0.9$) a third to 40\% of the weight moves to always-rest; one tick above break-even at $\rho_z=0.9$ half of it does, because the rotation rent of the tick is worth more than selecting. Nobody learns to stay out. The learned incumbents earn $0.03$--$0.10$ per period at break-even against $0.00$--$0.03$ for the fringe, with 15--26\% informed fills against the fringe's 26--31\%: they select.
\item \emph{Identity is worth more against incumbents that select, not less.} Paired over seeds, id $-$ anon is $+0.05$ to $+0.06$ per period at break-even, $\rho_z=0.9$, at every lag (five of five seeds; against always-resting incumbents with the same fringe it is $+0.03$ to $+0.04$). At $\rho_z=0.99$ it grows with the lag from $0.00$ to $+0.06$. The anonymous entrant's edge over always-resting shrinks with the lag ($+0.04\to+0.01$ at $\rho_z=0.9$): once the incumbents read the tape, an entrant that reads only the tape is one more of them, and the wallet tag is what is left.
\item \emph{The fringe pays.} With an always-resting entrant the fringe earns $+0.00$ to $+0.03$ at break-even; with a selecting entrant, $-0.02$ to $-0.04$, and $-0.10$ at $\rho_z=0.99$. Against no-regret incumbents at $\rho_z=0.99$ the always-resting \emph{entrant} is itself under water ($-0.04$ at $L=1$). The identity-blind resting liquidity is the counterparty of the selection rent---the structure the per-maker data show (\S\ref{sec:l2}).
\end{itemize}
The spread game and the queue game give the same answer through different actions: a quoter who knows who just printed captures the benign flow and leaves the toxic flow to identity-blind rivals---by undercutting selectively where undercutting is possible, by resting selectively where it is not.

\subsection{Anonymity as a venue lever: liquidity in stress (RQ3)}\label{sec:anonymity}
Give the no-regret incumbents an identity expert and they take it (weight $0.70$--$1.0$). The venue-design consequence appears when there is no uninformed fringe, so that whoever is at the inside is a selector (Figure~\ref{fig:liquidity}; five seeds, break-even).

\begin{figure}[h]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_liquidity_identity.pdf}
\caption{Liquidity in the toxic state when the incumbents see the tape only vs wallet identities (no fringe, break-even). Left: probability that an uninformed taker is filled in the toxic state. Right: what an always-resting, identity-blind maker earns among those incumbents. Five seeds, 95\% intervals.}\label{fig:liquidity}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Under anonymity, the mark lag is what keeps liquidity in the toxic state.} With the tape only and a lag of five or more arrivals the incumbents cannot tell a toxic spell from a calm one in time; they stay (many learn to always rest), an uninformed taker is filled 71--75\% of the time in the toxic state, and the incumbents earn nothing: they are the counterparty. At $L=1$ they withdraw as sharply as identity-aware makers.
\item \emph{Identity removes the lag, and the liquidity with it.} With wallet identities the incumbents withdraw in 62--72\% of toxic-state periods at any lag; the uninformed taker's fill probability in the toxic state is $0.23$--$0.30$ against $0.71$--$0.75$ under anonymity ($\rho_z=0.9$, $L\ge5$), and the incumbents earn $0.12$--$0.14$ per period instead of $\approx0$. When regimes are long ($\rho_z=0.99$) even a stale tape catches them and the two converge. The venue's choice is sharpest exactly where the entrant's identity edge is: regimes short relative to the lag.
\item \emph{Whoever is identity-blind on an identity book is the sole counterparty.} An always-resting maker earns $-0.13$ to $-0.29$ per period among identity-aware incumbents, against $+0.02$ to $+0.03$ among anonymous incumbents with a stale tape; with a fringe present, the fringe's loss doubles once the incumbents can see identities. The entrant's own identity edge survives identity-aware incumbents and grows ($+0.04$ to $+0.07$, five of five seeds): the anonymous entrant is then the worst-informed selector on the book.
\end{itemize}
Anonymity is a transfer from makers to uninformed takers in stress---takers keep access and the makers who provide it earn nothing---and public identity is the reverse. Neither defends the incumbents' rent; the lever decides who holds the toxic flow when nobody wants it. One qualification from the book (\S\ref{sec:l2}): Hyperliquid's top makers do not withdraw from the inside after a stressful spell at any timescale we can measure; they are absent from the toxic \emph{wallets'} fills while keeping their share of everyone else's. The withdrawal here is what incumbents do whose only regime signal is the tape; the data say the earning makers have the wallet signal and use it fill by fill, so on this venue the lever's effect would run through the identity-aware channel of the right-hand panel. This is the venue-side statement of the mark-lag mechanism of \S\ref{sec:identity}.

\subsection{The priority fee (RQ3)}\label{sec:fee}
A third action, rest-and-pay: payers are served ahead of non-payers, time priority within each group, the fee $f$ charged per period of priority whether or not filled, collected by the venue. A selector pays when it would rest, is not already first in time priority, and $P(\text{fill})\cdot v(\alpha_{\mathrm{next}})>f$; the incumbents' no-regret sets gain a paying variant of their selector. Five seeds, $\rho_z\in\{0.9,0.99\}$, $L\in\{1,5,20\}$, break-even, fringe of two; Figure~\ref{fig:fee}.

\begin{figure}[h]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_priority_fee.pdf}
\caption{The priority fee: profit per period, net of fees, of the identity entrant that uses the fee, the incumbents, and the fringe, and the venue's revenue, against the fee; incumbents that see the tape only (left) and wallet identities (right). Break-even, means over $\rho_z$ and $L$, 95\% seed intervals.}\label{fig:fee}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{An all-pay auction for position.} Every selector pays, in the calm state: tape-only incumbents in 66--77\% of calm periods and 18--27\% of toxic ones (they cannot tell), identity-aware incumbents in 93--94\% of calm and 8--9\% of toxic periods (they can). A non-paying identity entrant among paying identity-aware incumbents gets 0.0\% of fills: if you do not pay you do not trade in calm periods. The fee replaces time priority as the selection instrument.
\item \emph{The venue's revenue-maximising fee leaves every maker at or below zero.} Revenue peaks at $f=0.5$: $0.41$ per period with tape-only incumbents and $0.62$ with identity-aware ones, two to four times the makers' total rent at $f=0$. At that fee the entrant and the incumbents earn $-0.04$ to $+0.05$ and the fringe $-0.14$ to $-0.20$; the excess over the dissipated rent is the fringe's loss.
\item \emph{A cheap fee is a gift to the best-informed.} At $f=0.1$ the identity entrant among tape-only incumbents doubles its profit ($0.10\to0.20$) by paying in 88\% of calm and 8\% of toxic periods; identity-aware incumbents facing a non-paying entrant double theirs ($0.11\to0.21$). Information converts into position at the price of the fee.
\item \emph{Incidence.} The fringe's loss triples ($-0.05\to-0.15$; $-0.09\to-0.21$) at any fee anyone pays: pushed behind every payer, it keeps the fills the selectors declined (informed share $0.29\to0.40$--$0.54$). Taker access is unchanged.
\end{itemize}
The priority fee is not a defence of the incumbents' rent---at the venue's optimum it dissipates all of it. It is a tax on selection whose burden falls on whoever cannot select, and whose beneficiaries besides the venue are the best-informed, at fees below the venue's optimum. A pay-for-priority queue turns the time-priority rationing of the selection rent into a price, and the price is paid by the identity-blind.

\subsection{The makers' own levers: the profit--resilience frontier (RQ3)}\label{sec:defences}
Twenty frozen cartels ($\bar\alpha\in\{0.3,0.5\}$, ten seeds each) wrapped in a defence and evaluated on the heterogeneous market alone and against six profit-maximising adversaries (undercut, markout, anon, id, oracle, and a \emph{learned} one: a tabular Q-learner with the incumbents' own algorithm and training budget, trained against each defended cartel and frozen) plus the damage-maximiser; 500 episodes; per-episode defender profit for the CVaR. Defences: randomise the quote ($\epsilon\in\{0.1,0.3\}$, $\pm2$ ticks); requote only every 2 or 5 periods; floor the quote at the break-even of the regime forecast from the tape or from wallet identities. Figure~\ref{fig:frontier}: $x$ is the defenders' profit alone, $y$ their worst-case profit under attack (minimum over adversaries per cartel), both in rent units.

\begin{figure}[h]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_defence_frontier.pdf}
\caption{The profit--resilience frontier of the makers' own levers. One point per defence; $x$: profit alone; $y$: worst-case profit under attack over the profit-maximising adversaries; 95\% intervals over ten cartels ($x$ intervals reflect cartel-to-cartel variation; the paired changes are in the text). Up and right is better.}\label{fig:frontier}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Speed is necessary.} Requoting every other period costs $0.11$--$0.17$ rent units of worst-case profit ($0.18\to0.07$ at $\bar\alpha=0.3$, $0.08\to-0.01$ at $0.5$); every fifth period, essentially everything ($\kappa_A=1.0$--$1.3$). The only answer to an undercut is to undercut back; a slow responder keeps the residual flow---the reverse of the Brown--MacKay coercion logic, because this adversary takes flow rather than disciplining a rival.
\item \emph{Noise is a cost.} Randomising the quote lowers profit alone by $0.02$--$0.06$ and buys nothing in the worst case at $\bar\alpha=0.5$; against the learned adversary, the one it is meant for, it adds $0.03$--$0.04$ at $\bar\alpha=0.3$ and nothing at $0.5$.
\item \emph{Identity-aware control is the one lever that improves both axes, and only where the toxic regime is decision-relevant.} At $\bar\alpha=0.5$ the identity floor raises profit alone $1.01\to1.02$ and worst-case profit under attack $0.08\to0.19$ (intervals $\pm0.03$, $\pm0.02$), the worst-case CVaR $-1.07\to-0.75$; the tape's forecast gets about 70\% of that, identity the rest. At $\bar\alpha=0.3$ the gain ($+0.04$) is not distinguishable from zero, as in \S\ref{sec:identity}: against $\sim11$-tick cartels the toxic state rarely bites. The floor is the identity entrant's rule, used by the defenders.
\item \emph{A learned adversary is a weak one.} The Q-learning entrant finds a policy worth $0.3$--$0.4$ rent units where the hand-crafted undercut-with-inference rules take $0.7$--$1.0$, and leaves the cartel half its rent ($\kappa_A\approx0.5$--$0.6$). $\kappa_A\approx0.7$ (\S\ref{sec:exploit}) is the exploitability by structured best responses, not an artefact of weak adversaries; generic RL with the incumbents' budget does worse than the rules---what an entrant needs is the undercut and a toxicity estimate, not a value function.
\end{itemize}
The makers' levers barely move the frontier: the best of them leaves the entrant 75--80\% of what it took before. The defenders' problem is not that the adversary learns their reaction function; it is that the adversary undercuts, and the only response to an undercut destroys the rent. The rent is not defensible by the makers. The venue's levers (\S\ref{sec:queue}--\ref{sec:fee}) do not defend it either; they decide who holds the toxic flow and who pays for position. Nothing in the game defends the rent---which is RQ3's answer, and the reason RQ3 is not this paper's centre.

\subsection{Synthesis}
The chain the proposal set out to test now reads, with numbers: learning makers earn rent ($\Delta\approx0.8$) that survives a best-responding entrant (the cartel re-forms around it; entrant keeps 79--96\% of the monopoly rent); the entrant's binding problem is fill selection, i.e.\ reading the toxicity regime; on Hyperliquid the regime is readable from the wallet tag $0.5$--$0.9$ bps before the price reveals it; and on the major books, where the spread cannot widen, fill selection is the only place rent can live. The queue game of \S\ref{sec:queue} closes the loop in the simulator: on a book where the spread cannot move, the identity entrant earns the selection rent and the identity-blind makers absorb the toxic flow. The book adds the second action the queue model needs: a fill at the touch pays one tick, but one order in ten walks the book and pays twenty, and on BTC and ETH those are the informed ones---depth shown behind the inside is where the toxic flow lands. The same step on real quotes is now in (\S\ref{sec:l2}): the makers who select fills earn $0.4$--$1.1$ bps per fill more than everyone else at the same price, persistently, while the average fill at the touch loses $0.3$--$0.6$ bps within ten seconds. And part of \emph{how} they select is now visible in prints alone: the earning makers take a $2.6$--$6.5\times$ smaller share of last night's toxic wallets' fills than of everyone else's, at every level of the tape and on fills that were not pick-offs, while never leaving the touch after a move. They select on the wallet, not on the tape. What the December L4 lifecycles add is the \emph{action} behind that---cancellation versus queue position versus depth---which prints cannot separate.

\section{Research questions, restated}
\paragraph{RQ1 (Executable value of identity).} Under $\Fanon\subset\Fid\subset\For$, what is an entrant's profit, and how does $\pi^{\mathrm{id}}-\pi^{\mathrm{anon}}$ depend on regime persistence $\rho_z$, type concentration $\kappa_\alpha$, the mark lag $L$, and the incumbents' quote relative to the toxic-regime break-even? \emph{Prediction (from \S\ref{sec:identity})}: the increment is zero when the toxic state leaves an undercut profitable, and otherwise grows with $L$ up to the regime's lifetime. \emph{Empirical counterpart}: the identity-given-anonymous-signal split by coin, against each coin's spread relative to break-even; and, now in hand (\S\ref{sec:l2}), the makers who earn the spread take $2.6$--$6.5\times$ fewer of last night's toxic wallets' fills than of the benign and unseen wallets', inside calm and stressed tape alike and on fills that were not pick-offs.

\paragraph{RQ2 (Exploitability, as measurement).} For each incumbent population, report $L_{\mathrm{BR}}$ under the damage-maximising attacker and under the attacker's own best response, and $\kappa_A$. The claim supported so far: $\kappa_A\approx0.7$ with at most a weak dependence on $C$ (180 populations, ten seeds); a rational entrant captures about two thirds of what it destroys and the rest passes to takers as tighter quotes. Open: whether attackers that target the \emph{reaction function} (steering, \cite{dss,brownmackay}) rather than the quote level break the flatness of $\kappa_A$.

\paragraph{RQ3 (Tick-constrained books; new).} On a one-tick book, model the maker's action as queue position and cancellation at the inside rather than spread width, with the priority fee as the price of position. Does the identity signal translate into realized spread for makers who use it, and is the resulting equilibrium a selection cartel---makers who leave the toxic flow to each other---rather than a spread cartel? \emph{Results so far} (\S\ref{sec:queue}--\ref{sec:fee}): identity is worth $+0.04$ to $+0.10$ per period at break-even against always-resting makers, $+0.05$ to $+0.06$ against incumbents that select on the tape, and $+0.04$ to $+0.07$ against incumbents that select on identities; the identity-blind resting liquidity absorbs the toxic flow in the simulator and on Hyperliquid (\S\ref{sec:l2}). Anonymity keeps uninformed takers' access in stress at the makers' expense; identity reverses it; the priority fee dissipates the selection rent to the venue at its revenue-maximising level and falls on the identity-blind. Of the makers' own levers (\S\ref{sec:defences}) only identity-aware control improves both profit and resilience, and only where the toxic regime is decision-relevant; speed is necessary and noise is a cost. Nothing in the game defends the rent. \emph{Empirical anchor}: per-wallet realized spread of the top makers vs the average resting fill (\S\ref{sec:l2}: $+0.4$ to $+1.1$ bps at the same tick, persistent across nights), and the top makers' avoidance of typed toxic wallets at the touch; the December L4 lifecycles will say which action---cancellation, queue position, depth---carries it.

\paragraph{RQ4 (Defences; extension).} Quoter levers (randomisation, hysteresis, budget reserve, identity-aware control) and venue levers (anonymity, tick, request allowance, priority mechanism), reported as a profit--resilience frontier so ``robust'' cannot mean ``withdrew''.
