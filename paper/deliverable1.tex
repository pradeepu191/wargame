\section*{Motivation}
Market makers (MMs) post bids and asks and earn the spread from impatient traders, while losing to \emph{informed} traders whose orders predict the next price move (adverse selection). Increasingly, MMs are learning algorithms. Three facts make this a trading-firm problem, not only a regulator's. (i)~Independent learning MMs drift to spreads above the competitive level without communicating \cite{contxiong,cfl}. (ii)~A spread above competitive is \emph{rent} an entrant can capture by undercutting, but the same wide spread is also the correct response to toxic flow: identical observable, opposite action. (iii)~Optiver reports that current LLM traders name adverse selection yet trade at negative expected value and fail to model how others react \cite{optiver}. The skill a desk wants---read the tape, decide whether competitors are soft or the flow is poisonous, and quote accordingly without becoming the exploited party---has no quantitative benchmark.

\textbf{Central claim.} Learning market makers earn predictable supra-competitive rents; the regularity that sustains those rents is itself a strategic vulnerability; public market and wallet information lets an adaptive entrant exploit it; robust quoting can partially restore the profit--resilience frontier. The pieces exist separately: learned rents \cite{cfl,contxiong}; strategic exploitation of learners in abstract games \cite{dss,brownmackay}; on Hyperliquid, persistent wallet histories carry adverse-selection information \cite{zhai} and disclosed flow changes impact \cite{barone}. Nobody has asked whether learned rents survive a best-responding rival, or turned wallet-level predictability into executable quoting under competition---the gap \cite{zhai} leaves open.

We study this on \textbf{Hyperliquid}, an on-chain central limit order book (CLOB) for perpetual futures with billions of USD in daily turnover. Every committed order, cancel, rejection, and fill carries a persistent \emph{pseudonymous wallet address}, with both counterparties and post-trade inventory recorded \cite{albers}. Hyperliquid is our empirical laboratory and calibration source; the venue mechanics in Section~\ref{sec:hl} are treatment arms, not individual novelty claims.

\section{Setting and Model}\label{sec:model}
\textbf{Operational setting.} One perpetual contract on a Hyperliquid-style CLOB. Time is indexed by committed blocks $t=1,\dots,T$, a tractable event-time approximation consistent with the venue's atomic state-update structure (blocks arrive on an irregular clock, median $\approx 68$\,ms, and order priority is continuous in the priority fee within the block logic \cite{hldocs}). An episode is $T=100$ blocks; learning runs across $10^4$--$10^5$ episodes. We write $m_t$ for the mid-price, $\theta_t$ for tick size, and $V$ for the fundamental (the composite index Hyperliquid marks against).

\textbf{Agents.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{Incumbent MMs} $i\in\{1,\dots,N\}$, $N\in\{2,3,5\}$. Learners (Q-learning, Hedge/no-regret, PPO). Action $a^i_t=(\delta^{b,i}_t,\delta^{a,i}_t,q^i_t)$: bid/ask offsets from $m_t$ on the tick grid, and displayed size. Objective: $\max \E\!\sum_t \gamma^t\big[\pi^i_t-\phi (I^i_t)^2\big]$ where $\pi^i_t$ is trading PnL net of fees and $I^i_t$ inventory. Resource constraints: inventory cap $|I^i_t|\le \bar I$ and a \emph{request budget} $B^i_t$ (Section~\ref{sec:hl}).
\item \emph{Strategic entrant (the trader).} One MM who observes the public tape, infers whether the spread is rent or toxicity, and best-responds. The protagonist; later evaluated as the ``robust quoter.''
\item \emph{Takers.} One arrival per block: informed with probability $\alpha$ (trades in the direction of $V-m_t$; on Hyperliquid this is empirically latency arbitrage against Binance/OKX mid-prices), else a liquidity trader with private valuation $m_t+L$, $L\sim G$. $\alpha$ is the \emph{toxicity} dial. In the identity treatment each taker carries a persistent wallet id whose past fills are public \emph{after commitment}: a maker at $t$ sees wallet histories through $t-1$, never the identity of the order about to hit it.
\item \emph{Predatory taker.} An informed trader who chooses \emph{timing} and \emph{size} against incumbents' learning dynamics (provoke a price war, then trade into it; strike when a competitor's budget is exhausted).
\item \emph{Nonstrategic disruption: liquidation flow.} With hazard $\lambda(\sigma_t)$ a forced market order of size $S\sim F$ arrives, routed to the book, then to a backstop vault \cite{hldocs}. Stress case: the 10 Oct 2025 cascade, with $\approx$\$10.3B of 24-hour liquidations attributed to Hyperliquid and multi-venue spreads $\sim30\times$ wider in the worst 40 minutes \cite{oct10}; we compute Hyperliquid's own spread/depth path from \cite{albers}.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{State and information.} $s_t=(\mathcal{B}_t, m_t, \mu_t, \{I^i_t,B^i_t\}_i, \sigma_t, z_t)$: the visible book, public belief $\mu_t=\E[V\mid\text{tape}]$ updated by Bayes' rule after each print, inventories, request budgets, a volatility state driving $\lambda$, and $z_t$ = public flow features (wallet histories in the identity treatment). Information sets are nested, $\mathcal{F}^{\text{anon}}_t\subset\mathcal{F}^{\text{id}}_t\subset\mathcal{F}^{\text{oracle}}_t$ (no ids / committed wallet histories / the latent flow type, an infeasible upper bound), which turns ``identity'' into a value-of-information experiment. Fills follow price--time priority; the taker hits the best quote; ties resolved by queue position. \textbf{Payoffs.} Per fill $\pi^i_t=(p-V)$ when selling, $(V-p)$ when buying, plus maker rebate $r$ (negative fee at high-volume tiers) minus taker fee $f$ for aggressive orders. We report quoted/effective/realized spread (markouts at 1, 10, 60 blocks), taker welfare, $|\mu_t-V|$, MM PnL mean and CVaR, and the best-response loss defined under RQ2. \textbf{Sequence.} Quote $\to$ taker/liquidation $\to$ match $\to$ print, belief and budget update $\to$ learn; at episode end $V$ is revealed. \textbf{Benchmarks (exact).} Competitive Glosten--Milgrom quotes on the grid (ask $=\lceil \E[V\mid \text{buy},s_t]\rceil_\theta$); joint-monopoly quotes; collusion index $\Delta=(\bar\pi-\pi^C)/(\pi^M-\pi^C)$ \cite{calvano}. \textbf{Simplifications.} One asset, unit taker size, risk-neutral MMs, exogenous taker mix, no cross-venue hedging, calibrated (not endogenous) liquidation sizes.

\subsection*{Why Hyperliquid: treatment arms and calibration}\label{sec:hl}
Prior collusion/RL market-making papers use stylized dealer games or anonymized equity data. Hyperliquid supplies mechanics that serve as treatment arms in the simulator and as calibration targets in the data:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Persistent wallet identity.} Every committed order carries a pseudonymous address (a principal may run several). MMs in an \emph{anonymous} equity book pay to signal identity and avoid sniping each other \cite{ccg}; Hyperliquid wallet toxicity persists across ten-day windows (rank corr.\ 0.52) and improves return forecasts out of sample \cite{zhai}. We measure whether MM wallets avoid each other, whether the best-quoter identity alternates (our learners' turn-taking signature), and whether entrants are punished. Anonymous vs.\ pseudonymous is a design arm.
\item \textbf{Request budgets.} Address-based limits grant one action per USDC traded plus a buffer; a throttled address gets one request per 10\,s; cancels get headroom; limits are per user \cite{hldocs}. Quoting capacity is \emph{earned by filling}, $B^i_{t+1}=B^i_t+g(\text{fills})-c(\text{updates})$. Nobody can decrement another wallet's counter, so the attack is \emph{induced self-exhaustion}: stimuli (e.g.\ flickering quotes, which do elicit responses \cite{brogaard}) that make a reactive rival's own policy burn its budget; defences are hysteresis, minimum quote lifetime, a reserve.
\item \textbf{Priced latency.} Cancels precede executable orders; for aggressive orders a priority fee buys $\approx45$\,ms/bp up to 8\,bp, for resting orders it buys queue position within a 400\,ms window \cite{hldocs}. For a maker, priority is costly \emph{queue-position control} (action component $\rho^i_t$); the arms race of \cite{budish} has an explicit price schedule.
\item \textbf{Tick grid.} At most five significant figures subject to asset-specific decimal limits, integers always allowed \cite{hldocs}; we compute the effective grid from the venue rule per asset and price region. Tick coarseness governs learned supra-competitive pricing \cite{ccp}; power-of-ten crossings are natural experiments.
\item \textbf{HLP-inspired fixed benchmark.} The protocol vault runs several undisclosed strategies with on-chain positions \cite{hldocs}; a fixed-spread/inventory-skew quoter \emph{calibrated to observable HLP behaviour} is the rubric's fixed-policy agent.
\item \textbf{Data.} Open Level-4 data: every order lifecycle for BTC/ETH/SOL in Dec 2025, all trades Oct 2025--Jan 2026 \cite{albers}; the public feed also tags every print with both wallets. Calibrates $\alpha$, $G$, $F$, $\lambda$ and validates simulated reaction functions against real MM wallets.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Relevant Literature}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Colliard, Foucault \& Lovo} \cite{cfl} (RFS 2026): Q-learning MMs in Glosten--Milgrom adapt to adverse selection but fail to learn competitive pricing because undercutting feedback is noisy; rents are larger when adverse selection is weak. \emph{We take} their environment and exact benchmarks as baseline and replication target; our contribution begins \emph{after} the rents emerge: learned policy $\to$ observable behavioural signature $\to$ strategic best response.
\item \textbf{Zhai} \cite{zhai} (2026): on 17.1B Hyperliquid L4 messages, wallet-level toxicity is persistent and wallet-history features improve one-second return forecasts out of sample; turning this into profit ``would require a dynamic execution and market-making model accounting for latency, queue priority, fill probabilities, fees, and inventory'' and is left open. \emph{We take} the validated signal and the terminology (wallet identity, not trader identity). \emph{We differ} by asking whether the signal survives competition from adaptive rivals and becomes executable.
\item \textbf{Deng, Schneider \& Sivan} \cite{dss} (NeurIPS 2019) and \textbf{Brown \& MacKay} \cite{brownmackay} (2026): the first proves no-regret learners can be steered by a strategic opponent; the second shows a fast, persistent reaction rule can coerce supra-competitive outcomes even against a learning rival. \emph{We take} the steering attacker and the view of learned punishment as algorithmic commitment. \emph{We differ} by instantiating both in a market-making game with adverse selection and asking whether the regularity that sustains rents is what makes them exploitable. Supporting: \cite{contxiong,ccg,barone,brogaard,chilenje,dgj}.
\item \textbf{How decisions are made today.} Hyperliquid's public documentation specifies matching, rate limits, priority fees and liquidation routing \cite{hldocs}; Optiver's blog describes the reasoning MMs expect of traders and where models fall short \cite{optiver}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Research Questions}
\textbf{RQ1 (Executable value of information).} What is an entrant's profit under nested information, $\pi^{\text{anon}}\le\pi^{\text{id}}\le\pi^{\text{opp}}\le\pi^{\text{oracle}}$: book and flow only; plus lagged wallet-level toxicity; plus an estimated incumbent reaction function; plus the latent flow type (upper bound)? \emph{Benchmarks}: competitive-GM quoting and a fixed-spread/inventory-skew heuristic. \emph{Measure} net PnL per unit volume, realized markout, market share, inventory risk and CVaR$_{0.05}$ across $\alpha\in\{0.1,0.3,0.5\}$, $N\in\{2,3,5\}$. \emph{Prediction}: rent is highest where toxicity is lowest \cite{cfl}, so a spread-width heuristic enters the wrong markets. This is the dynamic-execution question \cite{zhai} leaves open.

\textbf{RQ2 (Does learned rent create strategic vulnerability?)} Characterise each trained incumbent population by its rent $C(\pi)=(\Pi(\pi)-\Pi^C)/(\Pi^M-\Pi^C)$; freeze it; compute the best-response loss $L_{\text{BR}}(\pi)=J(\pi;\text{nominal})-\min_{\pi^A}J(\pi;\pi^A)$ (the market is general-sum, so incumbent loss, not attacker gain, is the exploitability statistic). \emph{Hypothesis}: $\partial L_{\text{BR}}/\partial C>0$---the regularity required to sustain rents makes the policy easier to manipulate. \emph{Attackers}, escalating: one-tick undercutter; a strategic learner exploiting reaction rules \cite{dss}; induced request exhaustion; a predatory taker; held-out best responses (PSRO), with $\mathcal{A}_{\text{train}}\cap\mathcal{A}_{\text{test}}\approx\emptyset$. \emph{Incumbents}: Q-learning, no-regret/Hedge, one deep-RL method, HLP-inspired fixed.

\textbf{RQ3 (Defences; extension).} Quoter levers: randomisation, update hysteresis, budget reserve, identity-aware toxicity control, inventory limits. Venue levers: anonymity, tick grid, request allowance, priority mechanism. \emph{Report} the frontier $(\mathbb{E}[\Pi_{\text{nominal}}],\,-\text{CVaR}_{0.05}(\Pi_{\text{attack}}))$ with quoted spread, fill rate and taker cost, so ``robust'' cannot mean ``withdrew''. \emph{Empirical anchor}: from \cite{albers}, $P(\text{maker requotes within } h \mid \text{rival undercuts by a tick})$ for active MM wallets vs.\ the learned agents' reaction functions; wallet features use prior observations only.

\section{Plan and Feedback}
\textbf{By Workshop 1 (deliberately narrow).} (i) Two tabular-Q incumbents in Glosten--Milgrom replicating \cite{cfl} qualitatively; (ii) exact benchmarks, $C(\pi)$, a collusion certificate; (iii) one markout-rule entrant vs.\ competitive GM at $N=2$, three $\alpha$; (iv) BTC spread, markout, arrival and toxicity statistics on a manageable Hyperliquid sample. Deep RL, PSRO, queues, cascades, priced priority and request exhaustion are later-stage work. \textbf{Rigor.} Common random numbers across policies; confidence intervals over seeds; CVaR with mean PnL; train at $\alpha$, test at $\alpha'$; held-out attackers. \textbf{Target.} arXiv preprint by mid-December as a milestone toward peer review.

\textbf{Feedback requested.} (1) Stylized Glosten--Milgrom (exact benchmarks) or queue-based LOB (realistic, approximate)? We lean stylized plus request budgets and priced latency. (2) Is induced request exhaustion in scope---legal on the venue, adversarial in spirit? (3) We read the 10--100 node guidance as met by $N$ incumbents, entrant, takers and attackers on one book, and would rather not add a multi-asset extension that raises dimensionality without sharpening the claim---acceptable?
